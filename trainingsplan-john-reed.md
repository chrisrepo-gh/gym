# Training Plan — John Reed, 3×/week, Hypertrophy

**Built for:** Fernando · 3 sessions/week · ~60 min · goal: muscle / body composition
**Status:** ~2 months of training, mostly machines · introducing free weights now
**Legs:** one hip-hinge on Day B; the rest stays with cycling and hiking
**Injuries:** none reported — no movement restrictions built in
**Exercise names:** taken verbatim from the John Reed app library, so you can search them in the app

---

## How the week is structured

Three upper-body sessions, rotating emphasis. Every muscle gets hit 2–3× per week, which is
what drives hypertrophy at this training age far more than long single-muscle sessions.

| Day | Emphasis | Free-weight content |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Push (chest, shoulders, triceps) | Dumbbell bench, dumbbell shoulder press, lateral raise |
| **B** | Pull (back, biceps, rear delts) + hinge | One-arm dumbbell row, stiff-leg deadlift, EZ-bar curl |
| **C** | Mixed — incline chest, back, arms | Incline dumbbell bench, hammer curl, overhead extension |

Order in the week: A – rest – B – rest – C – rest – rest (e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri).
If you shift days around, just keep one rest day between sessions.

| 2 | Lat Pulldown Machine | 3 × 10–12 | 90 s | machine |

| 7 | Abdominal Machine | 2 × 10–15 | 60 s | machine |

**20 working sets** — this is now the longest session. If you run short on time, the
Abdominal Machine goes first, then a set off the Cable Reverse Fly. Never the hinge.

The stiff-leg deadlift is the one leg movement in the plan. Start very light — an empty-ish
pair of dumbbells for the first two sessions. The limiting factor is keeping a flat back
while the hips travel backwards, not the load. If you feel it in the lower back rather than
the hamstrings, you are rounding: reduce the range until you feel it in the hamstrings.

| Muscle group | Sets/week | Target range |
|---|---|---|
| Chest | 11 | 10–20 ✓ |
| Back (lats + mid-back) | 12 | 10–20 ✓ |
| Side delts | 6 direct + pressing | 8–16, low end ✓ |

| Biceps | 6 direct + all rows/pulldowns | 6–12 ✓ |
| Triceps | 6 direct + all pressing | 6–12 ✓ |
| Hamstrings / glutes | 3 | minimum viable — see note |
| Core | 7 | 4–10 ✓ |

Side and rear delts sit at the low end on purpose. Add a fourth set to the lateral raises
once you're comfortable with the session length — that's the first thing I'd increase.

Three sets of hinge per week is the floor, not a leg programme. It protects the posterior
chain and gives the hamstrings and glutes a genuine stimulus; it does not replace squatting
or leg pressing. Called out so you know exactly what you're getting for those seven minutes.

## Warm-up (8 minutes, every session)

1. 3–4 min easy cardio (bike, rower) — just enough to feel warm
2. Arm circles, band pull-aparts or Cable Reverse Fly with very light weight, 2 × 15
3. First exercise of the day: 2 ramp-up sets at ~50 % and ~75 % of your working weight

Ramp-up sets don't count as working sets.

---

## How to progress

**Effort per set:** stop 1–2 reps short of failure. You should believe you could do
1 or 2 more with clean form. On the last set of the last exercise you can go to failure.

**Adding weight — double progression:**

1. Start at a weight where you hit the *bottom* of the rep range on all sets.
2. Each session, try to add reps.
3. When you hit the *top* of the range on **all** sets, increase the weight next session
   (+2.5 kg on machines/barbell, +2 kg per dumbbell, or the smallest increment available)
   and drop back to the bottom of the range.

**Log every set.** Weight and reps, in the John Reed app or a notes app. Without a log
you're guessing, and progressive overload is the whole mechanism here.

**Deload:** every 6–8 weeks, take one week at ~60 % of your usual weights, same sets and reps.

## Introducing free weights — the actual point of this plan

You're coming from machines. Free weights add a stabilisation demand machines don't train,
which is why we're adding them — but that demand also means the first weeks are about
movement quality, not load.

**Weeks 1–4:** dumbbells and EZ-bar only, as written above. Deliberately light. On each new
free-weight exercise, start with a weight you could do for 15 reps and use it for your
8–10 rep sets. It will feel too easy. That's correct — you're building the motor pattern.

**Weeks 5–8:** load normally, per the progression rules above.

**After ~8 weeks**, if the dumbbell work feels controlled, swap in barbell versions:

- Dumbbell Bench Press → **Benchpress** (use a spotter or a rack with safeties)
- Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press → **Standing shoulder press**
- One-Arm Dumbbell Row → **Narrow-Grip Barbell Row** or **Wide-Grip Overhand Barbell Row**

Exercises in the app's library I'd deliberately **not** use yet: Clean & Jerk, Cleans,
Snatch, Push Press, all the kettlebell ballistic work, and anything "behind the neck".
These are technical lifts with a real injury cost and no hypertrophy advantage over what's
in this plan. Come back to them later if you want them for their own sake.

## Note on the leg exercise

You accepted the minimum version, so it is in the plan: one hip hinge, Day B, three sets.
That covers hamstrings, glutes and the lower back, and it transfers directly to cycling and
hiking. What it does not cover is the quadriceps — cycling loads them but does not grow them.
If you ever want that gap closed, a leg press or hack squat on Day A for 3 × 10–12 is the
whole fix, and it costs another seven minutes. Not a recommendation you need to act on now;
just so you know which door is still open.

## On the barbell progression

You confirmed the Chest / Free Weights list is complete: Close-Grip EZ-Bar Bench Press,
Dumbbell Bench Press, Incline Dumbbell Bench Press, Incline Dumbbell Fly. No flat barbell
bench there.

That does not block the week-8 barbell swap. The app files the barbell stations under the
**Machines** filter, not Free Weights — **Benchpress**, **Incline Bench press** and
**Decline Benchpress** are all in the Chest / Machines list. So the barbell bench exists at
the club; the app just categorises it by station rather than by implement. Same for
**Standing shoulder press** and the barbell rows, which sit under Shoulders / Free Weights
and Back / Free Weights respectively.

---

*One line on nutrition, since it determines whether this plan works at all: muscle gain
requires a small calorie surplus and roughly 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of bodyweight per
day. Training without that is pushing against a closed door. Ask if you want this worked out
properly.*
